What the README reports

Five things, each with a number and how it was measured

Training curves or runs

At least two measured runs, and what you read from each.

Test results

Per class or per language, on a test you can call fair, with three failures and a likely cause each.

The simpler alternative

The same test for both. The difference in items as well as in points.

Latency and size

Median time of one request on a laptop processor, and the size of the model file.

Licences and limits

The licence and provenance of every model and dataset, with its address, and the limits written for the person who reads the output.

The template has nine sections that cover these five things, plus how to reproduce every number. "In items" means: 14 photographs, one query, five drafts, as practised all week. Every model and dataset goes through the licence and provenance check of Lab 4.4; text written by a model is shown as a draft with its limits note from Lab 4.3.

Full marks, criterion by criterion, are described in the capstone brief. Reproducibility: one command per number, fixed seeds, the lock file committed, and the reviewer's numbers within the tolerance the README states. Authenticity evidence: the training logs and the commit history.

The defence

8 minutes to present, 4 to answer, one change made live

1 · The task and the model

Which use case, what you built, one decision you rejected.

2 · What you measured

Against the simpler alternative, on the same test. Latency, size, licences.

3 · Where it fails

Three failures with a likely cause, and the limits you wrote.

In the questions. The assessor picks one curve or one failure for you to explain, and names one hyperparameter. Say what will happen, change it, run, and explain what came out.

The live change is the authenticity check of the entry: a learning rate, the number of unfrozen blocks, a temperature, the number of results. Choose one whose effect shows within a minute in quick mode. A prediction that turns out wrong and is then explained well scores as high as a right one: what is assessed is the reading of the result.

Before the first defence

Clone it clean · 15 minutes

Clone your public repository into a new folder and run one command from its README. If that fails here, it fails for the reviewer.

Press T to start the timer. The clean clone catches most reproducibility losses: a data file that was never committed, a path that exists on one laptop only, a seed that was not fixed. Breaks are not slides: press B when the room needs one.
