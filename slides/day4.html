The simplest choice

Greedy decoding takes the most likely token, every time

  • The same prompt gives the same text: five drafts of a product were one draft
  • "Temperature 0" is the usual name for it
  • Useful where a result must be repeatable: tests, extraction, the ten tasks of Lab 4.2

What the lab measured

At temperature 0, 25 of 100 drafts still broke a rule: every one of them was longer than 20 words.

What goes wrong. "Temperature 0, so the model is right." Greedy decoding makes an output repeatable. It does not make it correct.

Concept

Instruction tuning is more training on conversations, with nothing added to the network

Base modelnext-token training on text
→
Instruction tuningthe same training, on instructions with good answers, wrapped in the template
→
Preference tuningpushed toward the answers people rated better
  • Same architecture, same number of weights: only their values change
  • The model learns the turn structure, to answer the request, and to end its turn
  • It is the fine-tuning of Day 2, applied to a language model

Concept level, as the entry says: nobody tunes a language model in this course. Building tuning data and adapters is AE-301, Day 3. The link to Day 2 is exact: a pretrained backbone, further training on a smaller, more specific dataset, weights that start from what was learnt. Sources: Ouyang et al., Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback (2022).

The other half of an answer

  • Generation ends when the model draws an end token, or at the limit of new tokens

Concept

Preference tuning shapes how an assistant behaves, not what it knows

  • People, or a model trained on their ratings, compare answers; the model is pushed toward the preferred ones
  • This is where tone, helpfulness, refusals and "I'm sorry, but I don't have that information" come from
  • It adds no facts: a learnt habit of declining is not knowledge of what the model knows

Why it matters this afternoon

In Lab 4.3 the model declines six questions it cannot know and answers two with invented facts. Both behaviours come from training, and neither is a check.

Concept level. The names participants will meet: reinforcement learning from human feedback, and direct preference optimisation; do not explain the algorithms. The practical consequence is what counts: two assistants built on similar base models can behave very differently, and behaviour changes between versions of the same model. Sources: Ouyang et al. (2022).

Concept

Reasoning effort buys quality on hard tasks with tokens you pay for and wait for

  • Some models write intermediate reasoning tokens before the answer; a setting says how much
  • They are generated like any other token: one run of the model each
  • They help on multi-step problems; a one-word extraction gains nothing

In the week's numbers

The small model wrote about 3 tokens a second on a laptop processor. At that speed, 300 reasoning tokens before a 19-token product line are well over a minute of waiting.

Not measured here. The lab's model has no reasoning setting. Treat effort as one more column of a model-choice table: quality gained against tokens spent.

Concept level, as in the entry; no lab measures it. The 3 tokens a second and the 19 tokens are from the measurements file of Lab 4.4 (one request at a time on a laptop processor). On hosted models reasoning tokens are billed as output tokens; say that the price list of the day decides, and give no figures.

The window

The context window counts everything: instructions, documents, question and answer

  • A fixed number of tokens the model can read at once; what is outside does not exist for it
  • The output is written into the same window
  • The model has a limit; the runtime is started with one too, often smaller, to save memory

In the lab

The runtime's window is 2,048 tokens. The shop policy and a question are 164 tokens. The policy, 110 catalogue lines and the question are about 3,400.

The model card of Qwen2.5-0.5B gives a context length of 32,768 tokens; the lab's runtime is started with 2,048, as a small local runtime on a laptop often is. Yesterday's count applies: about 86 English or 62 Arabic descriptions fill it. Attention's cost grows with the square of the number of tokens (Day 3), which is why a window is a budget and not a free setting.

What to do about it

Count the tokens before you send, and fail loudly

Count

Tokenise the prompt with the model's own tokeniser (Lab 3.1) and compare with the window minus the room for the answer.

Refuse or shorten on purpose

An error is a fault you can see. If you shorten, you choose what goes, not the runtime.

Know your runtime

Some return an error for a prompt that is too long; some cut silently, from the start or from the end. Test it once.

A silent cut is worse than a crash. It gives a confident wrong answer that nobody can trace.

The third check-yourself question of the lab. Do not name which products cut and which refuse: it differs by version and by setting. The habit is to test it with one prompt that is too long on purpose, as the lab does. Selecting what goes into the window (chunking, search, citations) is AE-203, Day 3.

Concept

A diffusion model makes an image by removing noise, step by step

Startan image of pure noise
→
Denoisea network predicts the noise; a little is removed
→
Repeattens of steps, each guided by the text
→
Imagea new picture that matches the text
  • Trained on images with added noise: the task is to predict the noise, a regression loss
  • One image costs many runs of the network, as one sentence costs many runs of a decoder
  • For the cooperative: catalogue illustrations, never a photograph of a product that exists

Concept level: no lab. Participants have met the parts: a convolutional or transformer network, a loss, a training loop, a text encoder that supplies the guidance. The responsible-use point is specific: a generated picture of a hand-made rug shows a rug nobody wove. Sources: Ho et al., Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (2020).

Concept

A vision-language model turns image patches into tokens a decoder can read

  • An image encoder cuts the picture into patches and gives a vector per patch
  • The vectors enter the language model beside the text tokens; attention does the rest
  • Image encoders of this kind are trained on pictures with their captions: matching pairs pulled together, as for yesterday's sentence embeddings

For Naqsh

A hosted vision-language model could be asked "which pattern is this?" with no training at all. Nobody has measured it on the cooperative's photographs, and the photographs would leave the shop's server.

Where the model runs

Open-weight or hosted: who runs it, and where the data goes

Open-weight, on your server

You download the weights and run them. The data stays. The version never changes unless you change it. You pay for the machine and the work of running it, and the licence is yours to read.

Hosted, behind an interface

The provider runs it. Much larger models than your server holds. You pay per token. The text or image leaves your server, and the provider updates and retires versions on its own calendar.

In the case. Anything that leaves the shop's server needs the sign-off of Bushra Al-Aqeel, the data protection officer, before a single test request.

Product descriptions are not personal data; customer queries and messages can be. The Personal Data Protection Law and its implementing regulation are the national references, listed in the course sources: point to them, and do not give legal advice from a slide. Serving an open-weight model as a monitored service is AE-205.
